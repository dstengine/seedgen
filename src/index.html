          How the seed phrase is made

          Everything runs in this page. The phrase comes from your device's own random generator, the addresses are derived on the spot, and nothing is sent anywhere — the page works with the network switched off, and once opened it opens again offline.

          The code is open: github.com/dstengine/seedgen. The derivation lives in src/wallet.ts, and the tests hold every chain to published reference addresses.

          A 24-word phrase is a standard BIP39 phrase that is also a valid TON mnemonic, so the same words open all five of those wallets. Monero has its own 25-word seed and wordlist, so the Monero row is a BIP39 import: the key at m/44'/128'/0'/0/0 becomes the spend key, the way Ledger and Ian Coleman's tool do it. Cake Wallet, Feather and Monero GUI will not read the phrase as a native seed; restore from the spend key instead. A TON mnemonic is always 24 words, so a 12-word phrase is BIP39 only: its TON address is the one Trust Wallet derives, and Tonkeeper will not take it.

          Trust Wallet takes any phrase from here, 12 or 24 words. It finds the same Ethereum, Bitcoin and Tron addresses, but it has its own paths for Solana and TON, so it shows those two at addresses of its own — the same coins are not in two places, they are two accounts. The card lists Trust Wallet's under "In Trust Wallet"; to see Phantom's Solana account in Trust Wallet, add it there with the derivation path set to Phantom's.

          Seed phrase generator: questions

          Is it safe to generate a seed phrase here?

          The phrase is made in your browser from your device's own random generator and is never sent anywhere: the page makes no network request and its security policy forbids one. Turn on airplane mode before you generate and it still works. The code is open on GitHub to check.

          How do I import the seed phrase into Trust Wallet?

          In Trust Wallet choose Add wallet, then I already have a wallet, then Secret phrase, and paste the 12 or 24 words. Trust Wallet shows the Ethereum, Bitcoin and Tron addresses listed on the card, and the Solana and TON addresses under In Trust Wallet.

          Why does Trust Wallet show a different Solana address than Phantom?

          Each wallet derives Solana from the phrase along its own path: Phantom uses m/44'/501'/0'/0' and Trust Wallet m/44'/501'/0'. Both accounts belong to the same phrase. The card shows both, so you know which address each wallet will open.

          Should I pick 12 or 24 words?

          Both are standard BIP39 phrases. A 24-word phrase from this generator is also a valid TON mnemonic, so it opens in Tonkeeper as well as in Trust Wallet, Phantom and MetaMask. A 12-word phrase is shorter to write down and works everywhere except Tonkeeper.

          Which wallets accept the phrase?

          Trust Wallet for Solana, Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron and TON (it has no Monero); Phantom, Solflare and Backpack for Solana; MetaMask and Rabby for Ethereum and other EVM chains; any BIP84 wallet for Bitcoin; TronLink for Tron; Tonkeeper and MyTonWallet for TON with 24 words; Monero wallets that restore from a BIP39-derived spend key for Monero.

          Does the generator work offline?

          Yes. Once the page has loaded it needs no connection, and after the first visit it opens offline too. It can also be installed as an app from the browser menu or opened as a Telegram Mini App.
